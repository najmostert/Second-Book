# Chapter 16: Sweden 2002: The Collapsing Library

We were off for a short Swedish summer holiday to visit our extended family and to attend my brother-in-law's upcoming wedding. We flew on a brand-new British Airways Boeing 777 aircraft with a very tight connection window before flying onwards to Copenhagen. The customs officials in Denmark were friendly which always significantly improves the start of any holiday. Tim wanted to try a local liquorice ice cream, which came topped with a thick layer of what appeared to be industrial salt. He promptly handed the cone over to me and because I hate wasting food, I was forced to chomp my way through this emetic disguised as ice cream, dry-heaving a few times into my napkin as we walked. 
Our train to Sweden arrived at the platform a short while later. A local girl took up the remaining fourth seat opposite us. We’d already had enough of international travel at this stage and just wanted to arrive at the apartment, but the journey perversely seemed to take forever with the three of us continuously nodding off to sleep and jerking upright every time the train came to a screeching stop at a station. We finally arrived at the large apartment where Anne-Sofie and Sven-Erik lived and we were shown to our rooms. The apartment was situated right in the heart of downtown Stockholm. Col and I were allocated the library room which featured a pull-out sofa bed, while Tim had another small bedroom down the hall. The family laughed at my cup of tea with the necessary two teabags to meet my tannin requirements. I detest weak, watery tea and usually travel with my own box of choice blend. We caught up in the large pink lounge, feeling fine until I noticed the time, at which point I instantly felt exhausted; the fact it was still light outside misled my internal clock. I closed the window blinds tightly to block out the summer midnight sun and settled down for the night. That is, until Col’s travel alarm clock squawked loudly at 3 a.m. We both sat up fast in the dark, trying to figure out which country we were in. Just then the frame of the pull-out bed collapsed beneath our weight and we ended up sleeping on the floor for the remainder of the night. 

Mushrooms and Bachelor Subterfuge

Breakfast was traditional Swedish fare: hårdbröd (crispbread), cheese, and a pungent fish roe paste that comes squeezed out of a toothpaste tube, served alongside tea. Col’s brother Mark and fiancé, Julie arrived shortly afterwards and ‘Jools’ had a great time playing with a water pistol with Tim before we drove out to the forest for a long walk under the tall fir trees. It was 27°C in the shade and I was forced to pay an expensive 5 Kronor fee to have a wee at a public toilet before chatting with Julie about her upcoming wedding. She was naturally a bit nervous that everything would work out on the day. I told her reassuringly that I had been forced to arrange my entire wedding in six weeks and it turned out beautifully. I think that relieved her anxiety a little. Afterwards we climbed the Kaknästornet radio tower to enjoy a spectacular 360-degree panoramic view of Stockholm. 
The following day, we drove 72 kilometres out into the countryside to stay at Sven-Erik’s summer cottage. The property was characterised by its traditional Falu red-painted timber exterior contrasted with bright white window and door frames. Two wooden houses sat next to the edge of a pristine lake. The toilet facility, located outside in a separate hut, consisted of a huge wooden box fitted with a toilet seat and a bucket of dry sawdust to scatter over your business. Our ablutions were conducted in a small chamber potty; the contents subsequently chucked onto a rapidly growing berry bush located directly behind the toilet hut. It was rustic, charming, and featured a large expanse of perfectly manicured green lawn. Everyone went for a cold swim in the calm lake whilst I lay back comfortably in a deckchair with a book. After breakfast, the family ventured into the dense forest to forage for wild chanterelle mushrooms—easily identified by their bright, golden colour—and wild blueberries. It was a lovely, serene walk, and I returned to the cottage with a basket filled to the brim with fresh mushrooms and berries. 
I woke up looking like the actor Vincent Schiavelli; swollen, baggy eyes and a bird’s-nest hairdo do not a confident woman make. After breakfast, Col and his mum left for Mark’s place to commence the elaborate subterfuge that was to culminate in his surprise bachelor party. The plan was that while Mark was distracted at the camera shop with Col, all his friends were to miraculously arrive at the counter and whisk him away for the remainder of the day. In the meantime, we ladies had some serious planning to do as well for Julie’s ‘Hen Night’. Julie was told that we needed to walk to the local train station under the pretence she was collecting a business parcel for her boss (personally that would have tipped me off). We set off on foot but Julie and I somehow managed to get completely lost. We couldn’t see where the rest of the girls had gone, so we stood waiting on a corner for them to return for us. At the terminal building—surprise!—all her friends were waiting in a group with backpacks and wide smiles. Julie seemed genuinely surprised by the ambush. We waved them off then the rest of us caught another train to the Science Museum. 

The Footpath to Karlskrona 

Col parked the car in the street in Karlskrona so we had to manually carry all our heavy luggage into the First Hotel. The hotel faced a pretty lane that featured very little traffic but many pedestrians. A welcoming holiday atmosphere smiled its way around the small coastal town. Col and I took a slow stroll around the area. Karlskrona is a beautiful, historic naval and fishing town. The local houses are constructed from red wood, flanked by beautiful gardens awash with bright summer colour. Sailing boats rose and fell gently with the tide and a fish processing factory stood near the shore. Later that evening, the extended family met up for dinner at a Chinese restaurant down the road. After the meal, everyone left for their respective hotels, leaving Col, Tim, and myself stranded on the pavement having missed the last l bus. Wi-Fi access was not available to look up a taxi so we had no option but to start walking back in the dusk—me navigating the tarmac in my high heels. Cars whizzed by at terrifying speeds, almost running us over on the dual carriageway and several drivers hooted angrily at us, as walking on the shoulder of the highway is naturally verboten. We arrived back at our cottage just before midnight, my feet in agony. 
The following morning after a lovely wedding ceremony, we gathered outside the stone arches to congratulate the beautiful bride and groom. Then followed a long line of guests down to the quay where a charter boat awaited us. At the reception hall, more formal photographs were taken before we headed inside and shown to our allocated tables. We had all been gifted a whimsical wedding memento with a personalised description written on it. Mine claimed that I owned a yellow Mini with no brakes! ( not true- I’d had them fixed). Col’s memento stated dryly that he was the pessimist of the family, happily married to an optimist. Mark clearly knows his brother well.
