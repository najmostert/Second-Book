# Chapter 24: Swaziland 2010

After making the first border from Mozambique into Swaziland by nightfall, we drove on into the waning light to a petrol station, filled the tank and by 7 p.m. found a lodge for the night just off the main road, which by Mozambican standards was a great improvement. The Lituba Lodge had a thatched roof with a large outer building that served as reception and dining room, all tastefully decorated with local basket ware. The owner was an affable Englishman who promised us three rooms. We had supper in the large open dining room while they prepared the rooms. We ordered steak and gazed at the huge TV screen against one wall where the US was playing against Ghana. The management and staff sat watching the game while we ate; it was the World Cup after all, I supposed. After supper we heard a ‘slight problem’ had occurred in that there were only two rooms with three beds available. The extra beds belonged to  permanent residents who were away for the weekend. They informed us that the sheets and duvets had been washed so there weren’t any spare. I'd frozen my backside off the night before with only one sheet as a covering and Swaziland (being at a much higher altitude) was a lot colder. Here we were again at 10 p.m. with only beds to sleep on and no bedlinen. We hauled the much travelled duvet out of the car which Col and I shared by pushing two beds together. Carl slept in his sleeping bag on the floor, Jax had the other bed and was wearing all her warm clothes and Tim only had our duvet cover and a jersey. As I climbed onto the hard bed, raucous laughter drifted up from outside announcing the arrival of a group of truck drivers and with it the futility of trying for sleep in any way other than the light and troubled kind. And so it was…

I awoke early to the sound of mooing. Thinking I’d mistakenly wandered into a barn during the night, I opened one eye to find a large bovine head staring at me through the open window. I got up quickly in the chilly dawn, and ventured outside to the sight of munching cows, clucking chickens and a braying donkey. It had been so dark the night before that we hadn’t noticed we were atop a mountain in the middle of a farm. Due to the lack of linen the manager reduced our rate and we set off for the Swazi/Zululand border. The border official said he liked Tim and Carl’s ‘Bob Marley’ pants and after a few deft stamps, we were in Zululand!

## Zululand

After leaving the Lavumisa border we headed towards Ndumu. The scenery gradually changed from cool, mountainous lushness to dry scrub with acacia trees lining the roadside. Mkuze game resort came into sight by nightfall. After unpacking the car, we set off for a game drive. It was great being in the bush again and everyone had fun game spotting. Jax spotted a white rhino walking steadily across the road. Rhino are being poached in their hundreds all over Africa and are endangered, which made this sighting all the sweeter.  The sky darkened with the promise of rain so we headed back just as it poured down and ordered takeaway toasted sarmies for supper from a man well in his cups. This resulted in the order missing most of the items and a second drive back to claim them from the heavy-lidded waiter.

The soft rain and misty conditions almost ruined any chance at game viewing the next day, but we did see a crowned eagle — one of the biggest; it can easily carry off a seven-year-old child. 

After leaving Mkuze we drove into the mountainous mists where visibility was very poor, yellow grass verges, red murram roads, green trees and grey skies. We later had better views of the mountains and Sterkfontein Dam. Navy blue skies against the buttercup yellow grass. Autumn colours abounded there with orange berry bushes, blue Maluti mountain range in sandstone and pale ochre. Distinctive pastel colours of the Free State.

We were driving towards Caledon when Col suddenly turned the Toyota onto a dry dirt road. He’d seen a couple of blue korhaan. They flew away as soon as they saw his camera. Col then decided he HAD to go into the Caledon Nature Reserve and we all groaned. At the gate he leaned out and in Afrikaans, asked the gatekeeper if he coiuld take a look.

We laughed at the man’s expression when he answered in good English that we might indeed visit. A few hours later we arrived back in Cape Town.
