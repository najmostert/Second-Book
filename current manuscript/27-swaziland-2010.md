# Chapter 24: SWAZILAND 2010

After making the first border from Mozambique into Swaziland by nightfall, we drive into the waning light to a petrol station, fill the tank and by 7pm find a lodge for the night, just off the main road, which by Mozambican standards is a great improvement. The Lituba Lodge has a thatch roof with a large outer building that serves as reception and dining room, all tastefully decorated with local basket ware. The owner is an affable Englishman who promises us three rooms. We have supper in the large open dining room while they prepare the rooms. We order steak and gaze at the huge TV screen against one wall where US is playing against Ghana. The management and staff sit watching the game while we eat; It’s the world cup after all I suppose. After supper we hear a ‘slight problem’ has occurred in that there are only two rooms with three beds available.The extra beds belong to some permanent residents who are away for the weekend. They inform us that the sheets and duvets have been washed so there aren’t any spare. I froze my backside off last night with only one sheet as a covering and Swaziland ( being at a much higher altitude) is a lot colder. Here we are again at 10pm with only beds to sleep on and no bedlinen. We haul the much travelled duvet out from out of the car which Col and I share by pushing two beds together. Carl sleeps in his sleeping bag on the floor, Jax has the other bed and is wearing all her warm clothes and Tim only has our duvet cover and a jersey. As I climb onto the hard bed, loud raucous laughter drifts up from outside announcing the arrival of a group of truck drivers and with it the futility of trying for sleep in any way other than the light and troubled kind. And so it was… I awaken early to the sound of mooing. Thinking I’d mistakenly wandered into a barn during the night, I open one eye to find a large bovine head staring at me through the window, I get up quickly in the chilly dawn and dress before venturing outside to the sight of munching cows, clucking chickens and a braying donkey. It was so dark last night, we hadn’t noticed that we’re atop a mountain and right in the middle of a farm. Breakfast is the usual fry up with toast and is quite tasty. Due to the lack of linen the manager reduces our rate and we set off for the Swazi/Zululand border. The border official says he likes Tim and Carls ‘Bob Marley’ pants, then after a few deft stamps, we’re in Zululand! 

ZULULAND 

After leaving the Lavumisa border we head towards Ndumu. The scenery gradually changes from cool, mountainous lushness to dry scrub with acacia trees. Mkuze game resort comes into sight by nightfall. After unpacking the car, we set off for a game drive. It’s great being in the bush again and everyone has fun game spotting. Jax spots a white rhino which is walking steadily across the road, providing a great view. Rhino are being poached in their hundreds all over Africa and are endangered which makes this sighting all the sweeter. At another hides there’sanother white rhino drinking at the water hole with a few Zebra and wildebeest. The sky darkens with the promise of rain so we head back just as it pours down and order take away toasted sarmies for supper from a man well in his cups. This results in the order missing most of the items and a second drive back to claim them from the heavy lidded waiter. The soft rain and misty conditions almost ruin any chance at game viewing today but we manage to see a Crowned Eagle - one of the biggest; it can easily carry off a seven year old child if it feels so inclined.There’s a Helmeted Guinea fowl, more Nyala, Impala, wildebeest and a warthog. I hear rather than see the hippo grunting repeatedly in the river. A ground Hornbill and Grey duiker completes the morning’s viewing. After leaving Mkuze we drive into the mountainous mists where visibility is very poor, then there’s yellow grass verges, red murum roads, green trees and grey skies. There are great views of the mountains and Sterkfontein Dam. Navy blue sky against the buttercup yellow grass. Autumn colours abound with orange berry bushes, blue Maluti Mountain range in sandstone and pale ochre. Distinctive pastel colours of the Free State. We’re driving through Caledon when Col suddenly turns the Toyota off onto a dry dirt road. He’s seen a couple of blue Korhaan. They fly away as soon as they see his camera. Col then decides he HAS to go into the Caledon Nature Reserve and we all groan. At the gate he leans out and in atrocious Afrikaans, asks the gate keeper if, ‘ Kan ons kan kyk!’ ( Can we can look?) We all laugh at the man’s expression, especially when he answers in good English that we may indeed visit. I shouldn’t mock Col’s attempts at speaking foreign languages, it’s not that bad, just that he always does so with his distinctive plummy ‘Rhodesian’ accent.
