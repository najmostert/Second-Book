# Preface

People often ask me, 'Where are you from?' I suppose my accent is hard to place, although I often get 'U.K.?' in response to their own question. As the years gallop by, I find it increasingly difficult to answer that question. I was born in England to a British mother and an Irish father, and I have lived in six countries, travelled to fifty, and attended thirteen schools. I was always the new girl, so I learned to adapt. In the UK, I had a British accent. I spoke like a Northern Irish person in Derry ('Whot are youse lukin’ aht') and adopted a softer Dublin accent while attending a convent in Ballycastle. I’ve had a Scottish accent and quite a strong Indian accent. I’ve lived in South Africa long enough to have picked up a good impersonation of a drunk 'bergie,' and taught anatomy to nurses in Afrikaans; no wonder my accent is not easily placed. This question got me thinking: where AM I from? Is it my place of birth, my parents, where I’m living now, or an accumulation of all the places I’ve lived? To help me answer this question, I’ve written my story, told mostly through travel, because I’m guessing this has definitely shaped a lot of who I am, if not the 'where.' A year before she died, I asked my mother to write about her experiences moving from the UK to East Africa as a young bride. She used an old pink Mac that I’d found for a bargain and began to type. Unfortunately, she didn’t get to finish, but I feel it’s only fitting that I begin her story as part of mine.
