# Chapter 21: Finland 2008

At breakfast — another groaning pile of possibilities — I settled for blueberries, raspberries and yoghurt, hardbröd and caviar and two large coffees.

A short while later we gathered ourselves to watch the Arabella glide gracefully into Helsinki harbour and dock, then disembarked with all the other passengers to wait in the weak sunshine for the tour bus to arrive. We had a buxom, square-faced woman as a tour guide. She spoke English fluently, alternating between Swedish, Finnish and English with ease. Our seats were very comfortable so we settled down for the hour and a half drive to the centre of Helsinki, stopping for forty minutes to look at the Temppeliaukio Church which is built into solid rock and bathed in natural light from the skylight surrounding the copper dome in the middle of the roof. The church is often used as a concert venue due to its excellent acoustics. I watched in amusement as an American family ‘Holy moly, willya look at that!’ their way past ancient relics.

Our next stop was Helsinki Cathedral (Tuomiokirkko), a beautiful and iconic landmark located in the heart of the city and dominating Senate Square. With its distinctive neoclassical architecture, stunning white façade, six slender columns and towering green domes, the cathedral has become a symbol of Helsinki.

Helsinki Old Church was next and we hopped off the bus to gaze at a beautiful, simple wooden Lutheran church dating from 1826. Senate Square, in the heart of Helsinki, is surrounded by the Cathedral, the Government Palace and lastly the University Main Building and National Library. After stopping at a few more places of interest, we ended up at a restaurant for a very expensive meal. The sky darkened and soon a light rain fell. The small group moved to another large craft store where I bought a few wooden butter knives. The rain stopped so we wandered through town and came to a huge outdoor market. They sold fisk, dried sausage, lots of berry jam, cheeses, olives, moose antlers, meats and — horrors — potted bear meat. I was not too happy to see that, it must be said.

The flower market was nearby so we took a wander over there. The market was a blooming riot of every vibrant colour imaginable, flowers, fruit and plump berries of every description.

We stopped at the well-known Fazer café, a Helsinki institution offering exquisite chocolates, pastries and a grand setting for a classic Finnish coffee break (fika). We ordered a cappuccino each and petit fours and sat at one of the pavement tables carefully watching the darkening sky.

Finnish and Saami crafts markets were nearby so we wandered over to take a look. Shelves displayed moose antlers, bone cutlery, colourful cloth, wooden cups (kuksa/guksi), bowls and other utensils often crafted from birch burls. There was traditional clothing (gákti) and footwear made from wool, cotton or reindeer skin, adorned with regional patterns, colours and silver or pewter details, and pewter thread embroidery used to decorate leather bracelets, garments and other accessories. So much to see, although we didn’t buy much, our weak currency being a deterrent.

It started to rain again while we were returning to the ferry. After showering on board we descended a few floors to try out another of their restaurants, this time à la carte. We confused the waitress by pouring our own water from the bottles she had provided, and Col poured his own wine while she looked on in consternation. I had pork and a small crayfish tail, Col went for the reindeer carpaccio and Tim declared his lamb the best he’d eaten. There was a small band playing jazz and later Tim and I went to the karaoke bar and listened to a few inebriated people torture some songs. We returned with hot chocolate for everyone.

I made coffee and went back to bed and slept till 8:30, then sat on deck and watched as we slowly approached the dock in Stockholm.

We were back at the country house. Tim skinny dipped and grazed his bottom jumping off the pier. I tried not to laugh at his pained expression. Anne-Sofie and I went for a forest walk to gather berries as women have done for millennia. The bushes were full of sweet blackberries and soon our fingers and mouths were stained purple with berry juice. My bucket was full so I returned, leaving my mum-in-law to gather some chanterelle mushrooms. I found lots more smultron (wild strawberries) on my way back so continued picking them for a while. I suddenly spotted a red deer a little into the forest clearing nibbling at some grass so stopped to watch it for a while before taking the road back to the holiday house.
