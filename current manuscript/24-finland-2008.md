# Chapter 21: FINLAND 2008

At breakfast,- another groaning pile of possibilities, I settle for blueberries, raspberries and yoghurt, hardbröd and caviar and two large coffees. A short while later we gather ourselves to watch the Arabella glide gracefully into Helsinki harbour and docks and disembark with all the other passengers to wait in the weak sunshine for the tour bus to arrive. We have a buxom, square faced woman as a tour guide. She speaks English fluently, alternating between Swedish, Finnish and English with ease. Our seats are very comfortable so we settle down for the hour and a half drive to the centre of Helsinki, stopping for forty minutes to look at the Temppeliaukio Church which is built into solid rock and is bathed in natural light from the skylight surrounding copper dome in the middle of the roof. The church is often used as a concert venue due to its excellent acoustics. I watch in amusement as an American family,’Holy moly, willya look at that!’ their way past ancient relics. Next stop is at Helsinki Cathedral (Tuomiokirkko), a beautiful and iconic landmark located in the heart of the city and dominating Senate Square. With its distinctive neoclassical architecture, stunning white façade, six slender columns, and towering green domes, the cathedral has become a symbol of Helsinki. Helsinki Old Church is next and we hop off the bus to gaze at a beautiful, simple wooden Lutheran church dating from 1826. Senate Square in the heart of Helsinki, is surrounded by the Cathedral, Government Palace, then lastly, University Main Building, and National Library. After stopping at a few more places of interest, we end up at a restaurant for a very expensive meal. The sky darkens and soon a light rain is falling. The small group moves to another large craft store where I buy a few wooden butter knives. The rain stops so we wander through town and come to a huge outdoor market. They sell fisk, dried sausage, lots of berry jam, cheeses, olives, moose antlers, meats and horrors - potted bear meat. I’m not too happy to see that it must be said. The flower market is nearby so we take a wander over there. The market is a blooming riot of every vibrant colour imaginable, flowers, fruit and plump berries of every description. We stop at the well known Fazer café; a Helsinki institution, this elegant café offers exquisite chocolates, pastries, and a grand setting for a classic Finnish coffee break (fika). We order a cappuccino each and petit fours and sit at one of the pavement tables carefully watching the darkening sky. Finnish and Saami crafts markets are nearby so we wander over to take a look. Shelves of Moose antlers, bone cutlery, colourful cloth, wooden cups (kuksa/guksi), bowls, and other utensils often crafted from birch burls display their ware. Traditional clothing (gákti) and footwear, made from materials like wool, cotton, or reindeer skin, and adorned with regional patterns, colours, and silver or pewter details. Pewter (tin) thread embroidery used to decorate leather bracelets, garments, and other accessories. So much to see although we don’t buy much with our weak currency as a deterrent. It starts to rain again while returning to the ferry. After showering on board we descend a few floors to try out another of their restaurant, this time it’s a la carte . We confuse the waitress by pouring our own water from the bottles she provided and Col pours his own wine while she looks on in consternation. I have pork and a small crayfish tail and Col goes for the reindeer carpaccio and Tim declares his lamb the best he’s eaten. There’s a small band playing jazz and later Tim and I go to the karaoke bar and listen to a few inebriated people torture some songs. We return with hot chocolate for everyone. I make coffee and go back to bed and sleep till 8:30 then sit on deck and watch as we slowly approach the dock in Stockholm. We’re back at the country house. Tim skinny dips and grazes his bottom jumping off the pier. I try not to laugh at his pained expression. Anne-Sofie and I go for a forest walk to gather berries as women have done for millennia. The bushes are full of sweet blackberries and soon our fingers and mouths are stained purple with berry juice. My bucket is full so I return, leaving my mum-in-law to gather some chanterelle mushrooms. I find lots more smultron ( wild strawberries) on my way back so continue picking them for a while. I suddenly spot a red deer a little into the forest clearing nibbling at some grass so stop to watch it for a while before taking the road back to the holiday house.
