# Chapter 34: AMSTERDAM 2014

I snap away at everything with my phone; whatever looks even vaguely photogenic. Old churches, the station building, the canal, ducks and boats. Our accommodation is above the pub which we find on the corner. The owner, George, is busy serving customers at the bar but eventually he stops long enough to give us two room key-cards. The entrance is next door up the narrowest stairs I’ve ever seen, to the second floor where the room leads off from the very small landing. The steps are half the usual depth and takes only half a foot at a time. It’s a struggle with the heavy backpacks as well. The room smells strongly of stale smoke and has three single beds and a bunk which Tim claims. After freshening up we walk sideways down the narrow stairs on tip toe to the busy walkway outside. Tim says we’re in the famous red light district. The streets are so full of mainly tourists, that you have to forcibly push past them to get up the street. I surreptitiously photographed the shops selling cannabis, the large windows featuring scantily dressed women, the canals, bridges, and old buildings. Tim left me to look for a friend. As I leaned over a bridge peering into the canal, a tourist approached and asked if I knew where the Red Light District was. I told him he was already in it and pointed vaguely toward the large windows and neon lights. I wandered past crowds chatting, watching, and laughing; many were openly smoking weed, which seemed very daring (it wouldn’t be legalised in South Africa for another decade). I popped into a tourist shop and emerged with an "I Heart Amsterdam" T-shirt, a couple of fridge magnets, and a free lollipop that must have contained something mind-altering, given its price and the nature of the shop. I was so disoriented coming out that I couldn't remember the way back to our accommodation. I awoke at 6 a.m. after a fair night, considering the noise levels hadn't dropped below 85dB. I wanted to photograph more buildings in the fresh dawn light, so I dressed quietly and walked sideways down the tiny, narrow steps. After a lovely hour exploring, I returned to a groggy Tim who was trying to get dressed—he had a pair of shorts on one leg and trousers on the other! We eventually made our way to the station, and I took in as much of Amsterdam as I could while we drove through the busy streets toward Schiphol Airport. Lunch on board was high in carbs and low in flavour. As we walked toward Customs, I suddenly remembered the "special" Amsterdam lollipop and started sweating. What if they tested it for narcotics? I tried to assume a casual walk and started a tuneless whistle—until I remembered that whistling is the international sign for "Search me, I’m carrying fifty kilograms of cocaine." My fears were nearly realised when an official called me over. I approached with a rictus smile and sweaty hands, but luckily, she only wanted to write our names in her little book.
