# Chapter 34: Amsterdam 2014

I snapped away at everything with my phone; whatever looked even vaguely photogenic. Old churches, the station building, the canal, ducks and boats. Our accommodation was above the pub which we found on the corner. The owner, George, was busy serving customers at the bar but eventually he stopped long enough to give us two room key-cards. The entrance was next door up the narrowest stairs I’d ever seen, to the second floor where the room led off from the very small landing. The steps were half the usual depth and took only half a foot at a time. It was a struggle with the heavy backpacks as well. The room smelled strongly of stale smoke and had three single beds and a bunk which Tim claimed. After freshening up we walked sideways down the narrow stairs on tiptoe to the busy walkway outside. Tim said we were in the famous red light district. The streets were so full of mainly tourists that you had to forcibly push past them to get up the street.

I surreptitiously photographed all the shops selling cannabis, the big windows that had various scantily dressed women posing, the canal and bridges and old buildings. Tim left me to go and look for his friend. As I was leaning over a bridge and peering into the canal, a tourist came up to me and asked me if I knew where the Red Light District was. I told him that he was already in it and pointed vaguely towards the large windows and flashing neon lights.

I wandered past crowds of people, chatting, watching, laughing, smoking; many openly smoking weed which seemed very daring (it wasn’t to become legalised in South Africa for another ten years). I wandered into a tourist shop and came out with a white T-shirt displaying the legend, ‘I heart Amsterdam’ as well as a couple of fridge magnets and a free lollipop that must contain something mind-altering given the price and the contents of the shop in general. I was already disoriented coming out from the shop and couldn’t remember the way back to our accommodation.

I awoke at 6 a.m. after a fair night considering the noise levels hadn’t gone below 85 dB all night. I wanted to photograph more buildings in the fresh dawn morning so dressed quietly and walked sideways down the tiny steps outside. After a lovely hour walking around the streets of Amsterdam, I returned to a groggy Tim who was trying to dress. He had a pair of shorts on one leg and a pair of trousers on the other. It was still quite hot and humid in the room. He eventually decided on shorts then we walked down to the station having negotiated the narrow stairs with great care. I was able to see as much of Amsterdam as I could as we drove through the already busy main road towards Schiphol airport.

Lunch on board was high in carbs and low in flavour. As we walked towards Customs, I suddenly remembered the ‘special’ lollipop from Amsterdam and started sweating. What if they found it and tested it for narcotics. I tried to assume a casual walk and started a tuneless whistle. Then I remembered, never whistle. It’s giving, ‘search me, I’m carrying fifty kilograms of cocaine in my handbag’.

My worst fears came true as an official called me over. I approached with a rictus smile and sweaty hands. Luckily she only wanted to write our names down in her little book and didn’t even ask to look in our bags.
