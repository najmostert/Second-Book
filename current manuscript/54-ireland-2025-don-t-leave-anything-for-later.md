The Book of Kells is an illuminated manuscript from the medieval period, created around 800 A.D. by Celtic monks, and contains the four Gospels of the New Testament in Latin. It is famous for its intricate, vibrant ornamentation and is considered one of the most significant cultural treasures of Ireland. They’re kept in Trinity College library where we are now.
The Chi Rho page of the book is the most celebrated decorated page from the book of Kells.
The book of Kells is written in Latin on vellum of calfskin and four main scribes had the task of drawing and decorating each page, so one scribe would write the words formally, the next scribe would colour, the next check for mistakes or imperfections. For small errors, a scribe would use a penknife to gently scrape away the top layer of the vellum where the ink had been applied. 
Most people in the 9th century were illiterate, so the visual imagery was essential for communicating the stories and messages of Christianity.

The rest of the family arrives around midday and there’s much shuffling of tables while we discuss our next move which turns out to be a visit to Newgrange, and at 5,000 years old, it’s the oldest building the world, built by Neolithic farmers around 3200 BC, making it older than Stonehenge and the Great Pyramids of Giza. It is famous for its unique alignment with the winter solstice, when the rising sun illuminates the central chamber. It’s thought it may have had one of the following purposes; as a tomb, ceremonial use, and as a spiritual centre, while later Celtic myths associate it with ancient gods and heroes. 
The structure is a large circular mound with an inner stone passageway and cruciform chamber (in the shape of a cross). Burnt and unburnt human bones, and other items of burial were found in this chamber. The monument has a striking façade made mostly of white quartz cobblestones and is ringed by engraved kerbstones. Many of the larger stones of Newgrange are painted or carved onto large rock or stones (megalithic art). The mound is also ringed by megalithic stones. 
Its initial period of use lasted around 1,000 years before gradually becoming a ruin, although the area continued to be a site of ritual activity. Excavations began from 1962 to 1975.
