# Chapter 36: Spain: Barcelona 2015

Barcelona was warm and damp, having recently rained. We took a bus to the hotel, checked in and went exploring, finding the Sagrada Família Basilica — a Gaudí building — and a Gothic cathedral called the Cathedral of the Holy Cross and Saint Eulalia before having lunch in Eixample. The Sagrada Família Basilica was a church still under construction, the largest unfinished Catholic church in the world. Designed by architect Antoni Gaudí, his work on the Sagrada Família had been added to an existing UNESCO World Heritage site in 2005. In 1882, Gaudí took over as chief architect, transforming the project with his architectural and engineering style, combining Gothic and Art Nouveau. Gaudí’s architecture was a visionary, organic, and revolutionary style that created fantastical and awe-inspiring structures. I kept expecting to see the head of a pixie or some gothic creature emerge from one of the windows.

That day we were looking around Gaudí’s Sagrada Família so I could take some more photos in the morning light before strolling through a long path in a park. Buskers played music on different instruments — sometimes using just their voices — and artists drew with chalk and watercolours on large canvases.

I made lunch with the provisions I had bought earlier and we sat on our tiny balcony munching on crusty French bread with cheese and ham. Afterwards, we took another walk through the park and watched a dark-haired woman painting space-like scenery on canvas with spray cans. She had short glossy hair that swung to and fro as she liberally sprayed her canvas of wood. She was very talented.

The huge, silver, bullet-shaped Torre Glòries, a 38-storey skyscraper, loomed in the skyline. Later that evening we walked to the Plaça Reial, found a jazz club and listened to an amazing jazz singer perform with three musicians. She had a statuesque figure and a powerful voice.

I admired some lovely glass windows at Casa Batlló, where sunlight hit and dispersed the vivid colours. As we strolled along the street in Plaça de Catalunya, Tim pointed out a little-known tile set among others on the walkway that featured Miró’s signature. I stooped to photograph it, amazed that he had noticed it as it was quite small. As we ambled along Las Ramblas, about halfway down the boulevard was the Mosaic de Miró, a colourful mosaic by the same world-famous artist, Joan Miró, who had lived in Barcelona and whose signature we had seen just half an hour before.

Playa de Barcelona was packed with sunbathers, most of whom had apparently thrown both caution and garments to the wind and were mostly naked. We passed by, looking conspicuous in our full attire, to find a nearby restaurant for lunch. Tim’s cousin, Kate Crowley, was in Spain studying Monet, so we arranged to meet her at our hotel. I bought some snacks, wine, and beers and when she arrived, we sat on the rooftop balcony and caught up.

Breakfast was at La Rambla that day. I took lots of photos of graffiti-covered walls; with plenty to choose from. In the evening, we went to a club called Opium. It was filled with purple glowing lights and a nice beat, so we shuffled around a bit, listening to the music.

That day we took a small plane back to Orly Airport in France then from Dubai arrived back in Cape Town where the cold of winter greeted us.
