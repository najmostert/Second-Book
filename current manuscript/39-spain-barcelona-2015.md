# Chapter 36: Spain: Barcelona 2015

Barcelona was warm and damp, having recently rained. We were exploring and looking for the Sagrada Família Basilica — a Gaudí building — and a Gothic cathedral called the Cathedral of the Holy Cross and Saint Eulalia before having lunch in Eixample. The Sagrada Família Basilica was still under construction; the largest unfinished Catholic church in the world. Designed by architect Antoni Gaudí, his work on the Sagrada Família had been added to an existing UNESCO World Heritage site in 2005. In 1882, Gaudí took over as chief architect, transforming the project with his architectural and engineering style that combined Gothic and Art Nouveau. Gaudí’s architecture was a visionary, organic and revolutionary style that created fantastical and awe-inspiring structures. I was expecting to see the head of a pixie or some gothic creature emerge from one of the windows.

I made lunch with the provisions I had bought earlier and we sat on our tiny balcony munching on crusty French bread with cheese and ham. Afterwards took another walk through the park to watch a dark-haired woman painting space-like scenery on canvases with spray cans. She had short glossy hair that swung to and fro as she liberally sprayed her canvas of wood. She was very talented.

I gazed up at the huge, silver, bullet-shaped Torre Glòries, a 38-storey skyscraper that loomed in the skyline. Later that evening we walked to the Plaça Reial, found a jazz club and listened to an amazing jazz singer perform with three musicians. She had a statuesque figure and a powerful voice.

I admired the stain-glass windows at Casa Batlló, where sunlight hit and dispersed the vivid colours. As we strolled along the street in Plaça de Catalunya, Tim pointed out a little-known tile set among others on the walkway that featured Miró’s signature. I stooped to photograph it, amazed that he had noticed it as it was quite small. As we ambled along Las Ramblas, about halfway down the boulevard we found the Mosaic de Miró, a colourful mosaic by the same world-famous artist, Joan Miró, who had lived in Barcelona and whose signature we had seen just half an hour before.

Playa de Barcelona was packed with sunbathers, most of whom had apparently thrown both caution and garments to the wind and were mostly naked. I felt conspicuous in our full attire, to find a nearby restaurant for lunch. Tim’s cousin, Kate Crowley, was in Spain studying Monet, so we arranged to meet her at our hotel. I bought some snacks, wine, and beers and when she arrived, we sat on the rooftop balcony and caught up.

Breakfast was at La Rambla after I took photos of graffiti-covered walls; with plenty to choose from. In the evening, I went with Tim to a club called Opium. It was filled with purple glowing lights, tanned youngsters and music with a great beat, so I shuffled around a bit.

The following day I returned to a wintery Cape Town.
