# Chapter 36: Spain: Barcelona 2015

Barcelona was warm and damp after recent rain. We checked into our hotel and went exploring, finding Gaudí’s Sagrada Família and the Gothic Cathedral of the Holy Cross and Saint Eulalia, before having lunch in Eixample. The Sagrada Família is the largest unfinished Catholic church in the world. Designed by Antoni Gaudí, its visionary, organic, and revolutionary style combines Gothic and Art Nouveau elements—I kept expecting a pixie or some gothic creature to emerge from one of the windows! The next morning, we returned to the Sagrada Família for photos in the morning light before strolling through the park. Buskers played an array of instruments, and artists drew with chalk and watercolours on large canvases. I made lunch with provisions bought earlier, and we sat on our tiny balcony munching crusty French bread with cheese and ham. Later, we watched a dark-haired woman painting space-like scenery on a canvas with spray cans; she was incredibly talented. That evening, the silver, bullet-shaped Torre Glòries loomed in the skyline. We walked to the Plaça Reial, found a jazz club, and listened to an amazing singer with a statuesque figure and a powerful voice. I also admired the vivid glass windows at Casa Batlló as sunlight dispersed through them. As we strolled along the Plaça de Catalunya, Tim pointed out a small tile set into the walkway featuring Joan Miró’s signature. We then ambled down Las Ramblas to see the Mosaic de Miró, a colourful work by the same world-famous artist. Playa de Barcelona was packed with sunbathers—most of whom had apparently thrown both caution and garments to the wind! We found a nearby restaurant for lunch, and later met Tim’s cousin, Kate Crowley, on our rooftop balcony to catch up. After a final evening at a club called Opium—filled with purple glowing lights and a great beat—we took a small plane back to Orly Airport, then flew to Dubai and finally back to Cape Town, where the winter cold greeted us.
