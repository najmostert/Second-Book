# Chapter 3: Namibia

The first few snags occurred when we arrived in Windhoek, having travelled first by train to Upington and then by bus. We were a travel-weary group when we were met by Dad’s sponsor. He took us to the house we would be living in and it was definitively not one of the promised new villas. On the contrary, it was one of the oldest houses in Windhoek and quite honestly, looked it. It had a tin roof, three bedrooms, and incredibly high ceilings. John had the room off the dining room, Mum and Dad took the other bedroom on that same side. Jacqui and Jo shared a room in the opposite ‘wing’. I ended up sleeping in the dining room with a large wardrobe pushed across the open archway to provide a modicum of privacy. The house was chilly in winter and because of the cavernous ceilings and open archways, it was terribly hard to warm up. There was a small fireplace with a round copper mantel which Dad later filled with bullet holes—ostensibly to create little holes to display semi-precious stones. The fact that he shot directly over Mum’s head to do so never occurred to him as bordering on madness. Such was the life we considered completely normal. I finished my schooling at Centaurus High School alongside John and Jacqui, while Jo attended the primary school just across the road. John later returned to Ireland to join the Irish Army. I started work as a student nurse at the State Hospital where Dad worked. It was a predominantly Afrikaans-speaking hospital; and since I had been exempt from learning the language during my last few years of school, I struggled immensely. Eventually, I decided to follow a boyfriend back to Cape Town and applied to work at the famous Groote Schuur Hospital, to complete my formal training.
