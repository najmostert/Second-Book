# Chapter 37: Turkey 2017: Istanbul

It was a significant birthday, so I decided to leave the country—and I felt Istanbul was far enough away. We flew Turkish Airlines and were lucky enough to have a two-seater row, which meant Tim had a window seat. I managed to fall asleep for four hours, but was awoken at 4 a.m. by the drinks trolley—a harsh start to the day. We arrived in Istanbul and registered for the airline’s free city tour. We were met by a guide who read out our names; we almost missed them as she asked if ‘Timot V R Mos’ and ‘Janeth’ were present! We walked with her and eight others to a bus, which drove us first to the Blue Mosque via JF Kennedy Road. We saw beautifully colourful wooden houses in yellow, blue, red, and green, and visited the Walled Obelisk, a Roman monument in the former Hippodrome of Constantinople. We were given a free breakfast at Turkistan Asevi: boiled eggs, olives, tomatoes, cucumbers, and cheese. It was a bit bland but welcome. The ‘Blue Mosque’ is actually the Sultan Ahmed Mosque. Its stunning interior is lined with over 20,000 handmade ‘Iznik tiles’—a turquoise-coloured traditional pottery. The upper levels are also painted blue, hence the name. It is the only mosque in Istanbul with six minarets; when first built, this provoked hostility because Mecca was the only temple with six. Sultan Ahmed I eventually paid for a seventh minaret for the mosque in Mecca to appease the community. Afterwards, we walked to the Haseki Hürrem Hamamı, a 16th-century Turkish bath, then passed the Bab-i-Hümayun and the walls of Constantinople. At the Topkapı Palace Museum, we findspectacular collections including Ottoman arms and the ‘Spoonmaker's Diamond’—a priceless 86-carat, pear-shaped stone. As we neared a large marketplace, the scent of spices assailed our nostrils, and I couldn't resist buying some dates. As the boarding call boomed over the speakers, we passed through passport control, received our gate passes, and settled into the departure lounge until 1 a.m. We were both exhausted, having endured an intense day of touring after a long, sleepless flight. As we shuffled up to the gate, I whispered to Tim that I was really looking forward to a good sleep on board. The hostess held out her hand but as I handed her my passport, she frowned and asked me to stand aside. When Tim handed her his documents, the same thing happened. We waited nervously, joined eventually by another anxious couple. When the last passenger had passed through, she turned to us and asked for our ‘Travel Cards’. We stared at her, dumbfounded. ‘We have British passports,’ I ventured. ‘We don’t need visas.’ She shook her head. ‘Not visa, Travel Card!’ None of us had even heard of such a thing. When I asked where we could get one, she replied, ‘You have to return to your country.’ I stared at her in disbelief. After an hour of frantic, fruitless searching for help—and being treated with indifference by officials in swivel chairs—the loudspeaker announced the final departure of our flight to Cuba. The holiday I had paid mucho pesos for to celebrate my birthday was dead in the water. It was 1:45 a.m., and we were beyond tired. ‘Let’s just find a coffee shop,’ Tim suggested calmly. After our luggage was unceremoniously booted off the plane, we spent the night trudging around the terminal until a café finally opened at 5 a.m. Tim took charge, booking us into the Hotel Sultania in Turkey for one night, followed by a flight to Croatia.

The Hotel Sultania was a vision of elegant restraint amid the crowded concrete jungle. After a much-needed nap, we explored the streets, eventually finding a lovely patio spot called The Olive Restaurant. As we sat there, the call to prayer rent the air with its eastern mystery, reminding me of Saudi Arabia. The waiter presented our meal with a theatrical tremble of the lid, revealing food that was both delicious and high in carbs. We wandered past the Hagia Sophia and the Hacı Beşir Ağa Mosque, with its characteristic white stone interlaced with flat red bricks. I purchased tickets for a sunset cruise along the Bosphorus, where we saw the New Mosque, the square Beylerbeyi Palace, and the beautiful Ortaköy Mosque. We arrived back at the dock around 9:30 p.m. to hunt for shisha before heading to bed. After a good sleep, we enjoyed a final breakfast at the Sultania, where the receptionist gifted us beautifully embroidered purses—I have used mine for my drawing pens ever since.
